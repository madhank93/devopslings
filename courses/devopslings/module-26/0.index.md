## What you'll be able to do

- Turn "copy an existing service" into a template that produces a new service
  with a green pipeline, a dashboard and an alert, and no manual step
- Write a guardrail as policy-as-code, with an exception path that expires on
  its own
- Give developers environments on demand that reclaim themselves, and an
  extension path that needs a reason rather than a ticket
- Route an alert from ownership metadata that lives with the service, and stop
  an unowned service at the pipeline
- Retire v1 of a paved road with thirty consumers on it, without a red build
- Define a platform SLI from the developer's journey rather than from the
  components' uptime, and compute its error budget
- Find in the data why two teams routed around the golden path, and fix the
  path rather than mandate it
- Stop one tenant's burst from starving a shared runner pool, and say why the
  mechanism you chose beats the two you did not
- Write a roadmap from adoption, toil and SLO data that says what it will not do

## The sandboxes

Four lessons run on `ci-stack`, the self-hosted forge and runner from module
12: <http://localhost:3000>, user `devops`, password `devopslings`. Five run on
`obs-stack`, from module 18: you work in its `box` container, Prometheus is at
<http://localhost:19090> and Alertmanager at <http://localhost:19093>.

The platform data in the `obs-stack` lessons — pipeline runs, developer
journeys, runner queues — comes from a small simulator that the lesson starts
in place of the payments gateway's exporter. It is a model, and each lesson
says what it models. The grader runs its own copy, so editing the simulator
proves nothing.

## Before you start

Module 12 for workflows and runners, module 18 for PromQL, recording rules and
Alertmanager routing, and module 24 for SLOs and error budgets. This module has
no intro rung: starting here is the mistake, not the on-ramp.
