## What you'll be able to do

- Predict who can read a given file, from the permission bits alone, and be
  right before you run anything
- Read a sudoers entry for what it actually grants rather than what it appears
  to say
- Find setuid binaries that do not need to be setuid, without breaking the ones
  that do
- Harden an SSH daemon while keeping the session you are working in alive
- Drop a service from root without stopping it from doing its job
- Read an AppArmor denial and widen a profile by the amount the denial asks for

## Why these

Hardening fails in one of two directions and both are expensive. Too little and
the machine is what the incident report is about. Too much and something legit
stops working, usually at the least convenient moment, usually with an error
that names none of the security machinery that caused it.

The habit this module builds is checking a rule against the mechanism rather
than against your expectation. A permission bit, a sudoers line and an AppArmor
profile are all precise; the surprises come from assuming they mean what they
look like they mean.

## Before you start

Module 01. This module runs `linux-box` — one machine with real systemd, real
users, and real permissions on real files.
